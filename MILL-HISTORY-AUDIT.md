# Mill History Page — Historical Accuracy Audit
**Source:** Ken W. Watson, *History of Old Stone Mill (Short Version)*, Delta Millers' Newsletter, Parts 1–6 (Fall/Winter 2023 – Spring/Summer 2026)  
**Audit date:** May 2026  

---

## Corrections Applied

### 1. Quote attribution (Chapter I pull-quote)
- **Was:** "— Traveller's account, 1817"
- **Now:** "— Rev. William Smart, *Statistical Account of Upper Canada*, 1822"
- **Source:** PDF explicitly identifies Rev. William Smart and the publication.

### 2. Jones & Schofield as "millwrights" (Chapter I)
- **Was:** "Loyalist millwrights" / "Its builders…were millwrights of considerable skill"
- **Now:** "local entrepreneurs" / "hired a skilled (but now unknown) millwright"
- **Source:** PDF states "We don't (yet) know the name of the millwright who built the Old Stone Mill." Jones was a merchant; Schofield was his business partner. Neither is identified as the millwright.

### 3. "Cevennes granite millstones" (Chapter III)
- **Was:** "a single run of granite millstones imported from the Cevennes by way of Montreal"
- **Now:** Removed. The source does not describe the original millstones in this way.
- **Note:** The French burrstones in use today were purchased from Upper Canada Village in 1995 and installed in 2004.

### 4. Red Fife timing (Chapter III)
- **Was:** "The grain it ground, almost from the beginning, was the variety we now know as Red Fife"
- **Now:** "Among the grains the mill ground in the later nineteenth century was the variety now known as Red Fife"
- **Source:** Red Fife was first selected by David Fife in 1842 — 32 years after the mill was built in 1810. "Almost from the beginning" is factually impossible.

### 5. Roller mill date and nature (Chapter III)
- **Was:** "By approximately 1905, roller mills had been installed alongside the millstones"
- **Now:** "By 1893, new owner George Haskin had replaced the millstones with a roller mill"
- **Source:** PDF: "Flour production using millstones had ceased in 1893 when then owner, George Haskin, replaced them with a roller mill." The millstones were replaced, not supplemented.

### 6. Delta Mill Society founding vs. incorporation (Chapter IV)
- **Was:** "In 1963 those trustees incorporated the Delta Mill Society…formally registered in 1972"
- **Now:** "In 1963 those trustees formed the Delta Mill Society…formally incorporated it as a non-profit charitable organisation on August 17, 1972"
- **Source:** PDF: "A deal was reached in 1963 and on August 28 Hastings signed the deed." / "On August 17, 1972 the Delta Mill Society was incorporated as a non-profit charitable organization."

### 7. Timeline 1980s — millstones claim (Timeline)
- **Was:** "Millstones restored to operation (1980s)"
- **Now:** "Mill opens to the public (1985)"
- **Source:** Millstones were not restored to grinding flour until October 2010 (Thanksgiving). The 1985 milestone was the first full public summer season opening.

### 8. Timeline 1963 — "Delta Mill Society is incorporated" (Timeline)
- **Was:** "Delta Mill Society is incorporated" at 1963 entry
- **Now:** Clarified: deed signed 1963, incorporated 1972
- **Source:** Same as #6 above.

### 9. Timeline 2010 — bicentenary entry (Timeline)
- **Expanded** to note that the millstones ground flour for the first time in over 100 years (October 2010 Thanksgiving).

### 10. Image — mill-1870-first-photo.png (Curator's Note sidebar)
- **Was:** `object-position: 50% 50%` — people in the photo were cut off in portrait crop
- **Now:** `object-position: 20% 50%` — shifts window left to show the people in the foreground
- **Note:** Image is 1013×754 px (landscape) displayed in a 3:4 portrait container. The people stand on the left portion of the frame.

### 11. Stat band — "Breastshot waterwheel" (Chapter III)
- **Was:** "Breastshot waterwheel"
- **Now:** "Wooden waterwheel"
- **Source:** The PDF does not identify the wheel type as breastshot.

## Debunked Local Myths (per PDF, not reflected in page — for reference)

The PDF explicitly debunks several myths that circulated in earlier DMS materials:
- The mill was **not** built on the site of Abel Stevens' original grist mill (it is ~50m away)
- The mill was **not** built in two stages with the upper storey added later
- The dam did **not** create Upper Beverley Lake — the lake pre-existed; the Old Stone Mill's 1810 dam enlarged it by an estimated 700 acres (mostly pre-existing swampland, not farmland)
- The millstone masons were **not** Jasper Russell and Isaac Whaley (both were born after 1810)
- John Beverley Robinson did **not** fund the 3rd floor construction

These myths are not present in the current page, so no action needed.

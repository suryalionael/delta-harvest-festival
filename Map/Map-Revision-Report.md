# Geographic Accuracy Report — "Throughout the Village" Interactive Festival Map

This report covers the rebuild of the interactive village map to reconcile **two sources of truth**: the official 2025 festival brochure map (`Map/Map.png`, event locations and visitor groupings) and real Delta, Ontario geography (roads, waterways, and building positions, sourced from OpenStreetMap and cross-checked against Google Maps and the coordinates the website itself publishes for the Old Stone Mill and Old Town Hall). This supersedes the geography section of all previous revisions, including an earlier version of this rebuild that used a non-uniform "scale break" to expand the tightly-clustered heritage core relative to outlying venues.

## Methodology

1. **Anchor point.** The Old Stone Mill (44.610103° N, 76.122493° W — confirmed via OpenStreetMap node `4795020556`) is the origin, plotted at canvas position (750, 480). Every other location's true compass bearing and straight-line distance from the Mill were measured in real-world metres, using an equirectangular projection local to Delta's latitude.
2. **Bearing preserved exactly.** Each venue is plotted on the 1600×1100 canvas at the *exact* real-world bearing from the Mill. None of the bearings below were adjusted for layout convenience.
3. **One uniform scale, no exceptions.** Real-world distance is converted to canvas units with a single constant: **1.3 canvas units per real metre**, applied identically to every venue, every road vertex, the lake shoreline, and the creek. There is no scale break and no per-venue expansion factor — a location 80 real metres from the Mill sits proportionally as close on the map as it actually is on foot, exactly like a location 500 metres away. This was a deliberate change from an earlier draft of this rebuild, which used a non-uniform radial scale that stretched the close-together heritage core apart for touch-target convenience. That approach was rejected because it made the map feel like an abstract diagram rather than the real village; the uniform scale below is the one actually shipped.
4. **Roads and water are real geometry, not illustration.** Every road centreline in the map (King Street/County Road 42, Mathew Street, William Street, John Street, Water Street, Mill Creek Drive, Recreation Drive, Lower Beverley Lake Park Road) and the Delta Creek millpond outflow stream are OpenStreetMap-surveyed point sequences, chained across way segments by matching shared endpoints, then run through the same bearing-preserving, uniformly-scaled projection. Lower Beverley Lake's near-shore is plotted from its real OSM shoreline relation (way `73895438`). Upper Beverley Lake's full shoreline could not be retrieved from OpenStreetMap (the Overpass API consistently rejected the query for it) and remains stylised in shape, though still positioned in its correct general direction and side of the village. Roughly 40 real OpenStreetMap building footprints near the village core are also drawn at the same scale, so the centre of the village reads as a real street grid rather than a handful of isolated venue markers.

## Known close marker pair

King Street and the Fire Hall Museum are the only pair placed with any judgment call, for the reason explained in the table above: they are genuinely about 30 real metres apart, and the uniform scale is not bent to create artificial separation. Both markers remain fully tappable, sit on real, OSM-surveyed road geometry (Mill Creek Drive), and never visually overlap on the rendered map.

## What this means for visitors

A Delta resident reading this map should recognise their own village: the Mill, Town Hall, Blacksmith Shop, and Russell Greenspace cluster the way they actually do, at the true tight scale a short walk represents; King Street runs where it actually runs; Lower Beverley Lake sits on the correct side of the village with a real shoreline shape; and the roads leaving the canvas point toward Athens/Brockville and Philipsville/Hwy 15 in their real directions. Because every venue, road, and waterway shares the exact same scale, distances on the map can be compared directly — if one venue looks twice as far from the Mill as another, it really is.

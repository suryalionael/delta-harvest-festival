The weekend

A weekend at Delta

Two days. Two historic spaces. One shared experience
of heritage, music, art, and community.

Saturday — Day 01
26 Sept · 10 AM — 3 PM

The annual Delta Harvest Festival fills the village. Blacksmithing demonstrations at the Blacksmith Shop, and milling at the Old Stone Mill — including a Red Fife wheat grinding on the 200-year-old millstones.

Sunday — Day 02
27 Sept · 9 AM — 5 PM

Start the day with breakfast and a learning corner, followed by four hours of music and line dancing at the Old Town Hall. Throughout the day, guests are invited to gather, connect, and enjoy a relaxed community atmosphere within one of the town’s historic spaces.

i.
Musician · Folk & Blues

Pat Johnson

1 PM — 2.15 PM · Solo, acoustic

With a guitar worn smooth at the second fret and a voice that sounds as if it has lived in this county for decades, Pat opens the afternoon. His set sits where a back-porch fingerpicker meets a delta blues bend — Canadian folk in the old, oral tradition, with the cracks left in on purpose.

Songs aren't museum pieces. You sing them so they keep walking.

ii.
Songwriter · French-Canadian & Contemporary

Erika Lamon

2.30 PM — 3.45 PM · Trio, with band

Erika sings in both French and English, and the seam between them is where her writing lives. Drawing on chansonnier tradition and a contemporary songwriter's sense of restraint, her set moves from intimate solo lines to fuller-band arrangements — small upright bass, brushed snare, a touch of fiddle.

Une chanson est un pont — entre deux langues, entre deux soirées.

iii.
Dancers · Interactive Line Session

Denzel & Jen

4 PM — 5 PM · Open floor, all ages

Closing the afternoon: a guided line-dancing session that is as much community ritual as country two-step. Denzel and Jen build the room from a slow grapevine to a full-floor cha-cha-cha by the final chorus — modern coaching, traditional steps, every body welcome.

If you can walk to the kitchen, you can line-dance. Start with a step. Add a smile.

The setting

A historic setting,
reimagined through art.

The mill has stood at the bend of the Lower Beverley for a hundred
and seventy years. For one weekend each September,
it becomes a stage, a gallery, and a long table.
